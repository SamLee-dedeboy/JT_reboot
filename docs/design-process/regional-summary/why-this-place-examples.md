# “Why This Place?” Example Copy

Draft copy for the regional summary cards. These examples explain why each place is relevant to a scenario’s strategy and potential impacts. They intentionally do **not** describe modeled salinity results.

## Bolster and Fortify

### North of Franks Tract

Just upstream of the new gates and reinforced levees, this area shows where these interventions could affect nearby channels.

### Franks Tract

Franks Tract is the scenario’s focal intervention area, where new operable gates and levee repairs could reshape drought-time water movement.

### Freshwater Corridor

This fortified corridor is intended to carry freshwater toward the southern pumping plants while keeping the route operable during drought.

### Clifton Court Forebay

Clifton Court Forebay is the corridor’s southern destination and shows how upstream interventions could affect water reaching the export system.

## New Green Watershed

### Suisun Marsh

Suisun Marsh shows how tidal-wetland restoration could support habitat, carbon storage, cultural practices, and more room for water as sea levels rise.

### Montezuma Slough

Montezuma Slough links proposed marsh restoration with Suisun Bay, showing how landscape change could affect water movement, habitat, recreation, and nearby land uses.

### Suisun Bay

Suisun Bay connects watershed-scale restoration with downstream estuarine habitat, where changes could affect fish migration, wetland connectivity, recreation, and shoreline communities.

### Confluence Zone

The confluence shows how watershed restoration, reconnected floodplains, and adapted Delta land uses could combine to influence water movement and ecological connectivity.

### Lindsey–Cache Slough

Lindsey and Cache sloughs show where wetland restoration could benefit habitat while affecting farming, flood management, and cultural access in the North Delta.

## Eco Machine

### Suisun Marsh

Suisun Marsh tests whether tidal restoration around Grizzly Island can redirect tidal energy while creating habitat, recreation, and eco-cultural opportunities.

### Montezuma Slough

Montezuma Slough connects restoration areas with Suisun Bay, revealing how restored landforms could redirect tidal energy through nearby channels and wetlands.

### Suisun Bay

Suisun Bay shows whether restoration-driven circulation changes in the marsh could extend to estuarine habitat, navigation, recreation, and adjacent shorelines.

### Confluence Zone

The confluence helps show whether changes from tidal restoration remain local or extend between Suisun Bay and the central Delta.

### Lindsey–Cache Slough

Lindsey and Cache sloughs show whether restoration could support North Delta fish and wetlands while changing flows experienced by nearby farms and communities.

## Increase Delta Outflow (+30%)

### Sacramento River Corridor

The Sacramento River Corridor shows how additional freshwater used to increase Delta outflow enters the Delta and affects downstream habitats, water users, and communities.

### Suisun Marsh

Suisun Marsh shows how stronger freshwater outflow could affect tidal habitat, managed wetlands, recreation, and surrounding communities and land uses.

### Montezuma Slough

Montezuma Slough shows how increased outflow could shift river–tide interactions affecting wetlands, fish movement, managed-marsh operations, and navigation.

### Suisun Bay

Suisun Bay is where stronger Delta outflow could benefit estuarine habitat while affecting shoreline uses, recreation, and regional water management.

### Confluence Zone

The confluence shows how a 30% outflow increase could affect the meeting of the Delta’s two major rivers and extend upstream or downstream.

### Lindsey–Cache Slough

Lindsey and Cache sloughs show how stronger outflow could affect North Delta habitat, farming, flood management, and community access.

## Calling on Reserves

### Sacramento River Corridor

The Sacramento River Corridor traces differently timed Shasta releases into the Delta, connecting reservoir operations with downstream habitat and water-management tradeoffs.

### Suisun Marsh

Suisun Marsh shows how differently timed reservoir releases could affect tidal habitat, managed wetlands, recreation, and water-dependent communities and land uses.

### Montezuma Slough

Montezuma Slough helps trace whether reoperating Shasta Reservoir could affect marsh channels and the habitats and activities they support.

### Suisun Bay

Suisun Bay tests how far changed releases might extend while highlighting the tradeoff between present estuarine benefits and future drought reserves.

### Confluence Zone

The confluence shows how changed Sacramento River releases could interact with San Joaquin River flow and influence the wider Delta.

### Lindsey–Cache Slough

Lindsey and Cache sloughs show whether better-timed releases could support tidal and floodplain habitat while affecting farms, flood management, and nearby communities.
